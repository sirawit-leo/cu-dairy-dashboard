Pick a cow above.

Both axes carry the same five weights — 8 · 7 · 5 · 3 · 2. Group A is always how long has this run, B how many times has it failed, C how severe, D what is permanently gone, E someone has already decided. Because the shape is the same on both sides, the two columns read in parallel and adding them means something.

Parity is not scored. The score says how bad the problem is; parity says how expensive it is to act on it, and a first-lactation cow has not yet repaid her rearing. Mixing the two would make a 7 unreadable — is the udder bad, or is she just old? So parity sits alongside as a column, and you weigh it.

One high reading is a bad day. A run is an infection that has settled in and is no longer being cleared between tests — which is what makes it a cow problem rather than an incident.

Repeat treatment is the cow-level failure signal. The relapse clause matters most: if the right drug was used and she came back anyway, drug choice is not the limiting factor and more treatment will not fix her.

22 cows · top 5
peak SCC ≥ 1,000 ×10002
peak SCC ≥ 5,000 ×10003

Peak says how bad the worst episode was; the mean says whether she sits high all the time. A cow can reach a high peak and recover — the two together separate a spike from a permanently high-cell cow.

3
D · Permanent loss
What will not come back?
6 cows · top 3
a blind quarter is recorded3
3 or more treatments to the same quarter2
teat end rough or very rough+1

Irreversible damage. A quarter treated three times is functionally lost whether or not anyone has written "blind" against it, and a damaged teat end keeps re-admitting bacteria however well she is treated.

These spread cow to cow at milking and cure poorly. This is the one group that is about the OTHER cows rather than about her.

Reproduction — 25

Is she failing to get back in calf, and is it hers to answer for?

8
A · Days open
How far past a 365-day calving interval is this lactation already?
20 cows · top 8
open at 150–200 days in milk3
open at 201–2505
open beyond 2507
and past 250 with services already used+1
confirmed pregnant0 — the clock has stopped

Every day open past roughly 115 DIM is a day added to the calving interval, and it cannot be recovered later in the lactation. This is the group that measures cost already incurred rather than risk.

7
B · Service failure
How many times has she been served without holding?
14 cows · top 7
3 services this lactation, still open2
4–5 services, still open4
6 or more, still open6
and the last pregnancy diagnosis was negative+1

"Repeat breeder" in the hard sense: served repeatedly AND failed. Services alone are not a fault — a cow served four times who is now pregnant scores nothing here.

5
C · Pathology
Is there a named physical reason she is not conceiving?
21 cows · top 4
uterine pathology this lactation, still open3
ovarian pathology, still open2
and retained placenta at the last calving+1

A named lesion is different from bad luck: it points at something treatable, or at something that will keep recurring. Retained placenta is scored as an add-on because it is a risk factor for the uterine disease rather than a separate failure.

3
D · Lost pregnancy
Has a whole lactation already been given up?
5 cows · top 3
abortion this lactation3
and more than one abortion on record+1

An abortion costs the lactation it interrupts and carries a recurrence risk. A second one on record is the part that changes the outlook.

2
E · Farm decision
Has the farm already taken her out of the breeding programme?
1 cow · top 2
flagged Not for Insemination2

The decision has been taken by someone who saw the cow. Only the exit date is still open, so this scores low on purpose — it confirms rather than discovers.

Fair-chance flags

These never change a score. They mark a breeding failure that looks like the farm's rather than the cow's, and they are shown only where there is a reproductive score for them to qualify.

F1
First service later than DIM 100

She started late because the farm started her late. Days open is being counted against a cow who was not offered the chance to conceive earlier.

F2
More than 60 days with no service while open and eligible

Nobody is trying. This is not a failure of the cow.

F3
Half or more of her services used the herd’s lowest-conception bull

Try Me S conceives at 19.2% against Try Me’s 43.8% (p = 0.034). Her failure to hold may be the straw, not the cow.

F4
Never served at all this lactation

There is no breeding failure to judge, because no breeding was attempted.

n/s beside a cow means her teat end was never assessed. That is a gap in the record, not a clean bill — and it caps group D below its maximum, so she cannot reach the top of the udder axis at all.

Before quoting anything here. The score is a ranking device built from the farm's own records; it is not a measurement of anything and it carries no economics — no cull price, no replacement cost, because neither exists in this project's data.
