The two health axes carry the same five weights — 8 · 7 · 5 · 3 · 2. Group A is always how long has this run, B how many times has it failed, C how severe, D what is permanently gone, E someone has already decided. Because the shape is the same on both sides, the two columns read in parallel and adding them means something. The economic axis is worth 30 and is weighted differently, because its groups are not equally large: days eating for nothing is over half of everything these cows cost.

Parity is not scored. The score says how bad the problem is; parity says how expensive it is to act on it, and a first-lactation cow has not yet repaid her rearing. Mixing the two would make a 7 unreadable — is the udder bad, or is she just old? So parity sits alongside as a column, and you weigh it.

This used to charge her the whole feed bill for every day past the 110-day target, as though she were eating for nothing. She is not — she is still milking. What a day open really costs is that she spends it at the tail of this lactation instead of near the peak of the next one, so the charge is the average day of a lactation minus what she actually gives that day, valued at ฿21.25/kg — and it is ฿0 while she is still above the average. Once she is dry and open she gives nothing, so the charge becomes the whole average day plus her feed. Every other component here is a cost. None of them ask the first question a culling list should: is she paying her way? This one does. Her recorded milk is set against Wood's curve for her own parity — the curve fitted to this herd — and the shortfall is valued at ฿21.25/kg. Two guards keep it honest. The expectation is summed only over the days she actually has a record, never over her whole days in milk, or the 81-day gap in the register would make every cow look like a failure. And the charge starts only 20% below the curve, not at it: the curve is fitted to this herd, so half the herd is under it by construction, and the herd’s own scatter is 19.6 percentage points. Charging from the line itself billed 36 cows ฿310,795 while ignoring ฿330,653 of surplus on the other side — the two cancel, and the herd is +935 kg against its own curve. Past the tolerance, 8 cows are charged and the worst is 39% under. A cow with fewer than 20 recorded days is not judged at all.

Why the discard window is six days and not fourteen. A ±14-day window around every high cell count and every treatment gives 26,566 kg of milk "not shipped" — 2.9× the 9,304 kg the farm's own ledger records as discarded, and 115% of the herd's entire income over feed cost. It happens because test rounds sit about 14 days apart, so the windows merge and blanket the year: one cow came out as unable to ship on 229 of 243 days. Treatment day plus six instead gives 8,176 kg, or 88% of the ledger, with the remainder covered by the five bloody-milk cases. Discarded milk also tracks the clinical case count month by month at r = +0.81, and colostrum does not confound it — that goes to the calves, in its own column of 18,241 kg.

Fair-chance flags

These never change a score. They mark a breeding failure that looks like the farm's rather than the cow's, and they are shown only where there is a reproductive score for them to qualify.

F1
First service later than DIM 100

She started late because the farm started her late. Days open is being counted against a cow who was not offered the chance to conceive earlier.

F2
More than 60 days with no service while open and eligible

Nobody is trying. This is not a failure of the cow.

F3
Half or more of her services used the herd’s lowest-conception bull

Try Me S conceives at 19.2% against Try Me’s 43.8% (p = 0.034). Her failure to hold may be the straw, not the cow.

F4
Never served at all this lactation

There is no breeding failure to judge, because no breeding was attempted.

n/s beside a cow means her teat end was never assessed. That is a gap in the record, not a clean bill — and it caps group D below its maximum, so she cannot reach the top of the udder axis at all.
